How the paper is made

Making Cedefop Daily

A morning paper on vocational education, skills and work, written from public sources and checked line by line by machine

Situation on Sunday 18 October 2026Edition No. 1 dated Monday 19 Octoberapprentix.eu/daily

Every weekday morning, Cedefop Daily gives Cedefop’s staff a five-minute read on what happened in their field: new Cedefop work, EU policy, sister agencies, research, the debate in the press across Europe, and life on the ground in vocational schools and workshops. It reads close to ninety sources in eleven languages, keeps only what bears on Cedefop’s own work, and will not publish a number, quotation or link that it cannot trace back to its source.

01 · Purpose

A paper made for one house

Most news services are written for everyone. Cedefop Daily is written for the people who work at Cedefop, in Thessaloniki and Brussels. Its subject is the agency’s own field: vocational education and training (VET), apprenticeships, skills intelligence, qualifications, adult learning and the future of work. It covers the EU institutions, sister agencies, member states and researchers working on these questions, and it looks beyond Brussels to how things are going in classrooms, workshops and companies.

Six principles govern every edition:

Relevant

If an item does not bear on Cedefop’s work, it does not run, however big the news.

True

Every claim is linked to its source. Numbers are copied, never recalculated into new claims. Quotations are verbatim.

Neutral

Evaluations are attributed to whoever made them. The paper never speaks for Cedefop and never says “we”.

Short

About thirteen items, readable in five to seven minutes, with links for anyone who wants more.

Automatic

Gathering, selection, layout, charts, dates and checks are done by software. Human time goes on judgement.

Discreet about people

Colleagues are never researched. People appear only in public roles, or with their consent.

06 · Process

The production line

Six steps turn the sources into a paper. Five are done entirely by software. The writing step is done by a writer working with a large language model, from the gathered text only, and its output is then held to the same mechanical checks as everything else.

Step 1

Fetch

Read all sources, tag each item by work area and policy, remove duplicates and add to the pool. About 30 seconds.

automatic
Step 2

Pool

One file holds everything the writer may use: about 1–3 MB of text a week, each item with its own identifier.

automatic
Step 3

Write

Story cards for the week, with headline, summary, body, priority and focus. Links are given as pool identifiers, never typed.

supervised
Step 4

Plan

Spread the stories across the remaining weekdays by priority and focus. No story runs twice; no day is overfilled.

automatic
Step 5

Render

Lay out the page, draw charts, write dates, sources and the “Why it matters” lines, and publish a list of the sources used.

automatic
Step 6

Check

Test every link, number, quotation, name, date and word against the rules. One failure stops the edition.

automatic

The writer never types a link

In a first draft, four press links were typed from memory, and all four were wrong. Since then, a story can only point to an item in the pool by its identifier. The software inserts the real address. A link that is not in the pool cannot appear in the paper.

What the software writes itself

The “Why it matters” lines, every date and source name, the four counts in “This week in numbers”, every chart and the list of sources are generated from data. The writer cannot change them, so they cannot drift from the facts.

A week’s bank, used with care

Stories are written once into a weekly bank and carry a use-by period: 3 days for policy, 7 for Cedefop news, 8 for the press, 30 for research and people. Lives profiles that are not used are kept in reserve for later Fridays.

One command per step

Each step is a single command, and each step’s output is a plain file anyone can open. A failed check leaves the previous edition in place.

08 · Verification

The integrity gate

Before an edition is published, a checking program reads it and compares it with the sources. It does not trust the writer, human or machine. When it finds a problem, the edition does not go out.

StopLinks

Every address on the page must come from the pool, or from a short list of the paper’s own pages. Every in-page link and file must exist.

StopNumbers

Every number in a story must appear in the text of its sources. Decimal commas and points are treated alike.

StopQuotations

Quotations must match the source word for word. Quotations from the press may not exceed 12 words.

StopBanned words

Promotional or loaded words may not appear outside a quotation (see the list below).

HoldSensitive subjects

Words such as audit, OLAF, ombudsman, litigation, harassment, disciplinary, fraud investigation, security incident or death hold the edition for an editor.

StopPeople

The same person may not appear in more than one headline. A name in a Lives profile must appear in its source exactly as printed.

StopDates

No past events in the diary, no consultations whose deadline has passed, no cultural pick outside Friday to Thursday. Every time and price must be on the event page.

StopCharts

Data must be present for at least ten countries in the chart’s year, every highlighted country must have a value, and ranked charts may not hold negative values.

WarnStyle and freshness

Headlines over 75 characters, stories past their use-by date, the Executive Director in a headline, a possible private name in a press story, more than 14 items on the page.

09 · Standards

The editorial code

Claims and numbers

  • Every claim is linked to where it comes from.
  • Numbers are copied from the source, never combined into new claims.
  • Evaluations are attributed: “Cedefop finds”, “the report argues”.
  • No anonymous sources, and no speculation about appointments or budgets.
  • Nothing pre-published: no drafts, no embargoed material.

Research

  • Papers are summarised from their abstracts, never from their titles alone.
  • Only what the abstract says is reported.
  • Preprints are labelled “not peer-reviewed”.
  • Paper of the day sets out question, method, finding and caveat.

Copyright

  • Press stories are summarised in the paper’s own words, with a link to the original.
  • Quotations from the press are limited to 12 words, in line with the press publishers’ right in Directive (EU) 2019/790, Article 15.
  • Articles behind a subscription are marked.

Style

  • Headlines are concrete, carry a verb and stay under about 70 characters.
  • Official EU names are used; acronyms are spelled out once.
  • Personal social media is never a source.
  • Translations are labelled with the original language.
10 · Privacy

People and privacy

A paper written for colleagues has to be careful about colleagues. The Daily’s rules were drawn up against the EU institutions’ own data protection regulation, Regulation (EU) 2018/1725, which applies to Cedefop in place of the GDPR.

Never

  • Searching the web for information about any member of staff
  • People-search sites or data brokers
  • Personal social media, family, health, appraisals, discipline, salaries, reasons for leaving, or rumour
  • Storing the names of staff who upload files to Cedefop’s website (the feed carries them; the software drops them)

Only in a public role

  • The Executive Director and staff acting publicly for Cedefop appear only in that role, and only when a public source names the person together with Cedefop.
  • The Director is capped at about three headlines a week, and the check warns when the Director is in one.
  • Office holders elsewhere are followed by role, not by name.

Lives, the one exception

  • A person can be named only as printed, in a profile they chose to give to a newspaper or magazine.
  • Adults only, and only what the story needs.
  • Never Cedefop staff, and no misfortune for its own sake.
  • The check confirms each name against the source.

Readers

  • No cookies, no tracking, no analytics.
  • The guess in Monday’s number game stays on the reader’s own device.
  • No staff e-mail addresses are held. If the paper is ever e-mailed, the proposal is a single message to a list that Cedefop controls.
  • Staff news is not part of the paper. It would come only through channels Cedefop approves, with consent and an opt-out.
11 · Conduct

Good manners with sources

Identified

Every request says who is asking and gives the project’s address. Nothing pretends to be a person in a browser.

No workarounds

When a website refuses programs, the refusal is respected. Its content is left out and the refusal is recorded.

Public about gaps

Sources the paper could not read are named on its “How it’s made” page, so readers know what it does not cover.

12 · Transparency

A public record of every edition

Every version is kept

If an edition is corrected after it appears, the earlier version stays online at its own address, with the stylesheets of the day, the edition’s data and its list of sources. No. 1 already has seven versions, from the first draft on 17 October to the final design on 18 October. Changes that only touch the look do not count as new versions.

Every edition lists its sources

Next to each edition sits a file with the items it used: 24 for No. 1, 15 for No. 2, 22 for No. 3, 16 for No. 4 and 22 for No. 5. Anyone can check any line against it.

An archive by date

The archive lists editions by date and never shows one before its day, in Athens time. The front page takes each reader to the edition for their day.

A laboratory

The same week’s stories are laid out in four alternative formats (a fixed shape or flexible sections, with or without the weekday rhythm), plus the earlier design. Editors can compare them side by side before deciding.

13 · Design

Design: a broadsheet in Cedefop blue

The design was chosen on 18 October 2026 from six mock-ups. It borrows the conventions of a quality newspaper: a centred masthead over a double rule, a small-caps dateline, columns separated by fine lines instead of boxes, and a colour for each section. The Daily has no Apprentix header, menu or footer. Its design lives in its own stylesheet, so a change to the Apprentix site cannot change the paper. 14 · Data

Built on Apprentix data

The Daily shares its home with Apprentix, an open site that gathers Europe’s apprenticeship and VET data every week and checks it before publishing. The Daily draws on it for its charts, numbers and quiz questions: 112 indicators with full provenance, Cedefop’s regional skills index for all 244 regions, portraits of the 34 apprenticeship schemes, policy changes from Cedefop’s timeline, and tested myths and facts. In the first week’s pool, 312 items came from Apprentix.

This is why the Chart of the day can show any EU-27 indicator against its target with Belgium and Greece highlighted, and why every figure in it already has a source, a licence and a date attached.

16 · Partnership

What Cedefop could add

The paper is built to run on public sources alone. A few things that only Cedefop can provide would make it better, and the system is designed to accept each of them.

An editor of record

A named person, for example in Communications, who sees the morning preview and can drop a story or stop an edition.

A data feed from Cedefop’s website

The website refuses programs, so the paper reads Cedefop through its news feed and the Publications Office. A feed or export would give fuller and faster coverage of Cedefop’s own work.

The events calendar

Read-only access to the internal calendar would switch on “Today at Cedefop” at the top of the paper.

Media monitoring

Access to the agency’s media-monitoring service would widen coverage beyond the 68 public press feeds.

The Data Protection Officer’s view

A record of processing and the DPO’s agreement before any staff news or e-mail delivery is added.

Alignment with the work programme

Matching the 11 work areas to the activities in Cedefop’s Single Programming Document, so the “Why it matters” line uses Cedefop’s own terms.

17 · Assessment

Why it is hard to copy

Facts checked by code, not by trust

Most AI-assisted writing relies on the writer being careful. Here, every link must exist in the gathered material, and every number and quotation is traced back to source text by a separate program that was tested with planted errors. Getting this right means designing the whole pipeline around it from the start.

Relevance that a machine can apply

Turning “what matters to Cedefop” into 11 work areas, 341 keywords in 13 languages, 10 policy hooks and 10 Cedefop products, so that every story explains its own relevance, takes knowledge of the agency’s field as well as programming.

Breadth without noise

Eighty-nine sources, from the Official Journal to trade papers in eleven languages, are filtered down to thirteen items a day that fit a five-minute read.

Ethics written into software

No staff research, names dropped at the source, sensitive subjects held automatically, copyright limits enforced, refusals respected and published. The rules are enforced by the program itself.

Editorial craft as rules

A weekday rhythm, use-by dates, a story bank that keeps every day supplied, a cap per person in headlines, and a laboratory for comparing formats. These are the habits of a news desk, written down precisely enough for a computer.

A full public record

Every version of every edition, with its sources, stays online. Few newsrooms publish this much about how each edition was made.

18 · Reference

Terms used in this paper

Pool
The week’s collection of everything gathered from the sources. The writer may use nothing else.
Bank
The stories written for the week, each with a priority, a focus and a use-by date, from which the software builds each day’s edition.
RSS, Atom
News feeds that websites publish so programs can see new items without reading the whole site.
API
A door a website offers to programs, so they can ask for data directly.
SPARQL
The query language of linked-data catalogues such as the EU Publications Office’s Cellar.
OpenAlex
An open index of research publications, used to find new papers that mention Cedefop or appear in VET journals.
Language model
Software that writes text by predicting likely words. On its own it can be wrong with confidence, which is why the Daily checks its output mechanically.
Integrity gate
The checking program that compares each edition with its sources and stops publication on any failure.
Bot check
A barrier some websites put up to refuse automated visitors. The Daily does not try to get past them.
Use-by date
How long a story stays fresh enough to run, from 3 days for policy news to 60 days for a Lives profile.
EUDPR
Regulation (EU) 2018/1725, the data protection rules for EU institutions and agencies.
