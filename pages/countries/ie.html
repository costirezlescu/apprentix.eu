Apprenticeships in Ireland

Statutory apprenticeship system

Craft apprenticeships follow a seven-phase on/off-the-job model, while post-2016 'new' apprenticeships extend the model to sectors from finance to biopharma, up to degree level.

CompanySchool

Around half

  • Pay Wage, paid by your employer or the state
  • Arrangement Ordinary employment contract
  • Duration 2–4 yrs (craft 4; consortium-led mostly 2)
  • Typical age 18–24
  • Leads to NFQ 5–10 (up to degree level) · access to higher education

What to expect on this scheme

You would most likely be between 18 and 24. Your training would be set out in a written arrangement (ordinary employment contract). You would spend about half your time at the company. You would earn a wage, set by collective agreements for your sector or by agreement with your employer, paid by your employer or the state; it also covers your days at school. It would take 2–4 years (craft 4; consortium-led mostly 2). You would finish with: NFQ 5–10 awards (EQF 4–8); craft: Advanced Certificate, NFQ 6, which gives direct access to higher education.

What is counted

Ireland's national figures are completions (apprentices qualifying each year) and their later employment, not registrations or a stock of apprentices in training. They include consortia-led apprenticeships up to NFQ level 8, whereas Eurostat's ED3SW is an upper-secondary enrolment proxy.
