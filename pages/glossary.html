Glossary

What the words and abbreviations on this site mean — VET, EQF, NEET, the flags after numbers and more — explained simply, for learners, parents and teachers as well as policy readers.

Across the site, the first time one of these terms appears on a page it has a dotted underline: hover over it, tab to it or tap it to see the definition. The definitions are short paraphrases; each links to the official source, which remains authoritative.

A

Adult learning
Courses, training or study that adults (usually 25–64) take part in, at work or in their free time.
Statistics distinguish formal learning (leading to a recognised qualification) from non-formal learning such as courses and workshops. Data often come from the Adult Education Survey (AES).
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: Adult education survey (AES) (paraphrased) · Related: Continuing VET (CVET), Validation of learning
Alternance (alternation)
Switching between learning at school and learning at a workplace, for example days of the week or blocks of weeks.
Cedefop calls this alternance training. Apprenticeship schemes differ in how the switching is organised: every week, in blocks of weeks or months, or a school period followed by a long company period.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Dual system (dual training), Apprenticeship
Apprentice pay (wage or allowance) also: apprentice compensation, allowances
What an apprentice is paid. A wage is taxable income from a job; an allowance is a payment that usually isn't.
Cedefop's fiches distinguish wages, allowances and reimbursement of expenses only. Pay may be set by law, by collective agreements or individually, and may or may not cover time at school.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Collective agreement, Learner status
Apprenticeship also: apprenticeships
Learning a job by working at a company and studying at school in turns, with a contract and pay from the employer.
In Cedefop's definition an apprenticeship is long-term, systematic training that alternates between a workplace and a school or training centre. The apprentice is linked to the employer by a contract, is paid (a wage or an allowance), and the employer is responsible for training that leads to a specific occupation. Countries use the word differently, so check each scheme.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Work-based learning, Dual system (dual training), Apprenticeship contract, Apprentice pay (wage or allowance)
Apprenticeship contract also: apprenticeship contracts, apprenticeship agreement, training agreement
The written agreement between an apprentice and the employer (sometimes also the school) setting out the training, time and pay.
Depending on the country it is an ordinary employment contract, a special apprenticeship contract, or another formal agreement. It may have to be registered with a school, ministry or chamber.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Learner status, Apprentice pay (wage or allowance), Chambers

B

Break in time series (flag b) also: break-in-series, break in series, time series break
Flag b: the way this number was measured changed this year, so don't compare it directly with earlier years.
A break can come from a new survey design, a new classification (e.g. ISCED 2011) or a change in definitions.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Flags

C

Candidate countries also: potential candidate countries, candidate country
Countries officially on the path to joining the EU, such as Albania, Montenegro or Ukraine.
'Potential candidates' (such as Kosovo) have been promised the prospect of membership but have not yet been granted candidate status.
Source: European Commission — EU enlargement (paraphrased) · Related: EU-27, EFTA
CC BY 4.0 licence also: CC-BY-4.0, Creative Commons Attribution
An open licence: you may reuse and share the data freely, as long as you credit the original source.
Source: Creative Commons — Attribution 4.0 (paraphrased) · Related: Provenance
Cedefop
The EU agency for vocational education and training: it researches VET and publishes data and reports on it.
Full name: European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training, founded in 1975 and based in Thessaloniki, Greece. It runs the databases on apprenticeship schemes, VET policies and key indicators used on this site.
Source: Cedefop — About (paraphrased) · Related: ReferNet, Key indicators on VET (KIVET), Eurostat
Centre of Vocational Excellence (CoVE) also: CoVEs, Centres of Vocational Excellence
A partnership of VET schools, companies and local bodies, funded by Erasmus+, that works to make vocational training top quality.
The European Commission describes CoVEs as networks that build local 'skills ecosystems', working with businesses on innovation, regional development and inclusion. Erasmus+ CoVE projects link such networks across several countries.
Source: European Commission — Centres of Vocational Excellence (paraphrased) · Related: Erasmus+, Vocational education and training (VET)
Chambers
Organisations of businesses (e.g. chambers of commerce, crafts or trades) that often register apprenticeship contracts and run exams.
In countries such as Germany, Austria and Luxembourg chambers check that companies can train, advise apprentices and organise final assessments.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Social partners, Apprenticeship contract
Collective agreement also: collective agreements
A deal negotiated between employers and trade unions that sets pay and working conditions, often for a whole sector.
In many countries apprentice pay is fixed in sectoral collective agreements, for example as a percentage of a skilled worker's wage rising each year.
Source: Eurofound — European industrial relations dictionary: Collective agreement (paraphrased) · Related: Social partners, Apprentice pay (wage or allowance)
Continuing VET (CVET) also: C-VET, continuing vocational education and training
Training adults take after they have started working, to update their skills, learn new ones or change job.
Continuing VET comes after initial education or after entering working life. It can be employer-provided courses, retraining for a new occupation, or adult apprenticeships.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Vocational education and training (VET), Initial VET (IVET), Adult learning
Council Recommendation on VET (2020) also: Council Recommendation on vocational education and training
An agreement of EU governments (24 November 2020) on making vocational education modern, flexible and good-quality.
Its full title is 'Council Recommendation on vocational education and training for sustainable competitiveness, social fairness and resilience'. It sets the EU's VET targets for 2025. A recommendation is not legally binding.
Source: Council Recommendation of 24 November 2020 on VET (EUR-Lex) (paraphrased) · Related: EU 2025 targets for VET, Osnabrück Declaration

D

Definition differs (flag d)
Flag d: this country measured the thing slightly differently from the standard definition; see the metadata.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Flags
Dual system (dual training) also: dual systems, dual VET, dual apprenticeship
A way of training where most time is spent working in a company, with regular days or blocks at vocational school.
The term is linked to the German, Austrian and Swiss models, where companies and schools share responsibility for training under national rules. Some countries use 'dual' for school-based programmes with longer company periods, so the label alone does not tell you how much time is spent at work.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Apprenticeship, Alternance (alternation), Work-based learning

E

Early leavers from education and training also: early leaving
18–24-year-olds who finished at most lower secondary school and are not in any education or training now.
Eurostat's definition: highest level attained is ISCED 0–2 and no formal or non-formal education or training in the four weeks before the Labour Force Survey. Shown as a share of all 18–24-year-olds.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: Early leaver from education and training (paraphrased) · Related: NEET, Labour Force Survey (LFS), ISCED
EFTA
The European Free Trade Association: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland — close to the EU but not members.
Source: EFTA — About (paraphrased) · Related: EU-27, Candidate countries
Employment premium
How many percentage points more often recent vocational graduates have a job than recent general-education graduates.
Cedefop's indicator compares 20–34-year-olds who finished 1–3 years before the survey at the same ISCED levels. A positive value means VET graduates are more often in work. Sometimes called the 'VET premium'.
Source: Cedefop — Key indicators on VET (paraphrased) · Related: General education, Percentage point, Key indicators on VET (KIVET)
Employment rate also: employment rates
The percentage of people in an age group who have a job (even a part-time one).
Eurostat follows the International Labour Organisation: anyone who did at least one hour of paid work in the reference week counts as employed.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: Employment rate (paraphrased) · Related: Employment rate of recent VET graduates, Labour Force Survey (LFS)
Employment rate of recent VET graduates also: employment of VET graduates
Of young people (20–34) who finished vocational school 1–3 years ago and are no longer studying, the share who have a job.
Eurostat measures it from the EU Labour Force Survey for people whose highest qualification is vocational upper-secondary or post-secondary (ISCED 35/45). The EU set a target of at least 82% by 2025.
Source: Eurostat — Employment rates of recent graduates (edat_lfse_24) (paraphrased) · Related: EU 2025 targets for VET, Employment rate, Labour Force Survey (LFS)
EQF level also: EQF levels, EQF 1, EQF 2, EQF 3, EQF 4
A number from 1 (basic) to 8 (most advanced) showing where a qualification sits on the European scale.
Most upper-secondary apprenticeship certificates sit at EQF 3 or 4; master craftsperson and higher VET qualifications are often at EQF 5 or 6. The same kind of certificate can be placed at different levels in different countries.
Source: Europass — Description of the eight EQF levels (paraphrased) · Related: European Qualifications Framework (EQF), National qualifications framework (NQF), Master craftsperson
Erasmus accreditation also: Erasmus accreditations
A long-term 'pass' that lets a school or company apply more easily for Erasmus+ money to send people abroad.
Accredited VET organisations (action KA120-VET) get simplified, regular access to Key Action 1 mobility funding based on an approved Erasmus Plan, instead of applying project by project.
Source: European Commission — Erasmus accreditation (paraphrased) · Related: Erasmus+, Learning mobility
Erasmus+
The EU's programme that funds study, training and work placements abroad, plus partnerships, in education, training, youth and sport.
In VET, Erasmus+ pays for learners, apprentices and staff to spend time abroad and funds partnerships such as Centres of Vocational Excellence. The current programme runs 2021–2027.
Source: European Commission — Erasmus+ programme guide (paraphrased) · Related: Erasmus accreditation, Learning mobility, Centre of Vocational Excellence (CoVE)
Estimated (flag e)
Flag e: the publisher estimated this value instead of measuring it fully.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Flags
EU 2025 targets for VET also: EU-level target
Goals EU countries agreed for 2025: 82% of recent VET graduates in work, 60% with work-based learning, 8% learning abroad.
Set in the 2020 Council Recommendation on vocational education and training. The 82% and 60% targets refer to recent VET graduates; the 8% target to VET learners with a learning mobility abroad. They are EU-wide averages, not targets for each country.
Source: Council Recommendation of 24 November 2020 on VET (EUR-Lex) (paraphrased) · Related: Council Recommendation on VET (2020), Employment rate of recent VET graduates, Work-based learning, Learning mobility
EU-27 also: EU27
The 27 countries of the European Union since the UK left on 1 February 2020. An EU-27 figure covers all of them together.
Eurostat publishes EU-27 totals for earlier years too, recalculated for today's 27 members so that trends are comparable.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: EU enlargements (paraphrased) · Related: EFTA, Candidate countries
Europass
Free EU online tools to describe your skills and qualifications, including a CV builder and a database of qualifications.
Europass also publishes the qualifications that countries register, with their EQF level, so they can be compared across Europe.
Source: Europass (paraphrased) · Related: European Qualifications Framework (EQF), National qualifications framework (NQF)
European Qualifications Framework (EQF)
A shared 8-level European scale that makes qualifications from different countries easier to compare.
The EQF describes each level by what a learner knows, can do, and how independently and responsibly they can work. Countries link their own national frameworks to it, so a certificate can show its EQF level. It is a translation tool, not a qualification itself.
Source: Europass — Description of the eight EQF levels (paraphrased) · Related: EQF level, National qualifications framework (NQF), ISCED
Eurostat
The statistical office of the European Union, based in Luxembourg. It publishes official, comparable statistics on EU countries.
Source: Eurostat — About us (paraphrased) · Related: Labour Force Survey (LFS), Cedefop

F

Flags also: publisher's flags
Letters after a number that warn you about it, for example b (break in series), u (low reliability) or p (provisional).
Flags come from the publisher (usually Eurostat or Cedefop) and are kept as published. Hover over or select a flag letter to see its meaning. A flagged value is still official, but compare it with care.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Break in time series (flag b), Low reliability (flag u), Definition differs (flag d), Provisional (flag p), Estimated (flag e), Not available ( : )

G

General education also: general stream
Academic school programmes (like a high-school track) that do not prepare you for one specific job.
Statistics often compare VET learners and graduates with those from the 'general' (academic) stream at the same ISCED level.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) (paraphrased) · Related: Vocational education and training (VET), Employment premium

I

Initial VET (IVET) also: I-VET
Vocational education taken before you start working life, usually in upper secondary school (around ages 15–19).
Cedefop uses IVET for vocational education and training in the initial education system. Statistics on IVET usually cover ISCED levels 3 and 4 (upper secondary and post-secondary non-tertiary).
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Vocational education and training (VET), Continuing VET (CVET), Upper secondary education (ISCED 3)
ISCED also: ISCED 2011, ISCED 35/45, ISCED 0-2, ISCED 3-4
UNESCO's international scale of education levels, from 0 (early childhood) to 8 (doctorate), used to compare countries.
ISCED 2011 levels: 0 early childhood, 1 primary, 2 lower secondary, 3 upper secondary, 4 post-secondary non-tertiary, 5 short-cycle tertiary, 6 bachelor's, 7 master's, 8 doctoral. Codes like ISCED 35 and 45 mean vocational programmes at levels 3 and 4.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) (paraphrased) · Related: Upper secondary education (ISCED 3), Post-secondary non-tertiary education (ISCED 4), Short-cycle tertiary education (ISCED 5), European Qualifications Framework (EQF)
ISCO also: ISCO-08
The International Standard Classification of Occupations: an international list grouping all jobs into numbered categories.
Maintained by the International Labour Organization. ISCO-08 has 10 major groups (1-digit) and 43 sub-major groups (2-digit).
Source: ILO — ISCO (paraphrased) · Related: Labour and skills shortage index (CLSSI)

J

Journeyman's certificate
The qualification you get for passing the final exam of a craft apprenticeship (in some countries called a trade certificate).
It shows you are a qualified skilled worker in your trade. In several countries it is the step before a master craftsperson qualification.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Master craftsperson, Apprenticeship

K

Key indicators on VET (KIVET)
Cedefop's set of statistics comparing vocational education across European countries, mostly based on Eurostat data.
Source: Cedefop — Key indicators on VET (paraphrased) · Related: Cedefop, Eurostat

L

Labour and skills shortage index (CLSSI) also: skills shortages
Cedefop's score (1 to 4) of how hard employers find it to fill jobs in an occupation; higher means bigger shortage.
It averages three component shortage indicators for each occupation group (ISCO 2-digit) and country.
Source: Cedefop — Labour and skills shortage index dataset (paraphrased) · Related: ISCO, Skills intelligence
Labour Force Survey (LFS) also: labour force surveys
A large EU-wide household survey asking people about their jobs and education. Many indicators on this site come from it.
Because it is a sample survey, small groups give less reliable numbers, which Eurostat marks with a 'u' flag.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: Labour force survey (LFS) (paraphrased) · Related: Eurostat, Low reliability (flag u), Employment rate
Learner status also: apprentice status
Whether an apprentice counts in law as a student, an employee, or a special mix of both.
Status affects pay, social security, holidays and protection at work. Cedefop's scheme fiches record which of these applies in each scheme.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Apprenticeship contract, Apprentice pay (wage or allowance)
Learning mobility also: mobility abroad
Spending time in another country to study or train, for example an apprentice placement abroad.
Cedefop's indicator counts IVET learners with a stay abroad of at least 10 days. The EU aimed for 8% of VET learners to have a learning mobility by 2025.
Source: Cedefop key indicators on VET — IVET learners with a learning mobility abroad (paraphrased) · Related: Erasmus+, EU 2025 targets for VET
Low reliability (flag u) also: low-reliability
Flag u: the number is based on a small sample, so it may be quite far from the true value. Treat it with caution.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Flags, Labour Force Survey (LFS)

M

Master craftsperson also: Meister, master craftsman
An advanced qualification for experienced crafts workers; in some countries it lets you run your own business and train apprentices.
Examples are the German and Austrian Meister. It usually follows an apprenticeship and work experience. Countries place it at different EQF levels, most often 5 or 6.
Source: Cedefop — National qualifications frameworks (NQF) online tool (paraphrased) · Related: Journeyman's certificate, EQF level, Apprenticeship

N

National qualifications framework (NQF) also: national qualifications frameworks, NQF level
A country's own official ladder of qualification levels, used to show how its certificates relate to each other.
Cedefop describes an NQF as an instrument for classifying qualifications by levels of learning achieved. Most European NQFs are linked to the EQF, but national levels do not always have the same number as the EQF level.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: European Qualifications Framework (EQF), EQF level
NEET also: NEETs
Young people Not in Employment, Education or Training: they have no job and are not studying or in any course.
Eurostat counts people who are not employed and had no formal or non-formal education or training in the four weeks before the survey, as a share of everyone in the age group (on this site often 15–29).
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: Young people neither in employment nor in education and training (NEET) (paraphrased) · Related: Early leavers from education and training, Labour Force Survey (LFS)
Not available ( : )
A colon ( : ) or a dash means there is no value: the data were not collected, not published or are confidential.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Flags

O

OECD
The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development: a group of mostly high-income countries that compares their policies and data.
Based in Paris. Its 'Education at a Glance' data are used on this site for some indicators, including for non-EU countries.
Source: OECD — About (paraphrased) · Related: Eurostat
Osnabrück Declaration
A 2020 promise by European education ministers, social partners and the Commission on VET actions for 2021–2025.
Endorsed on 30 November 2020. It puts the Council Recommendation into practice through four objectives: quality and flexible VET; continuing VET and digitalisation; a green link in VET; and an international European education and training area.
Source: Osnabrück Declaration (2020), hosted by Cedefop (paraphrased) · Related: Council Recommendation on VET (2020), Continuing VET (CVET)

P

Percentage point also: percentage points
The plain difference between two percentages: going from 60% to 66% is a rise of 6 percentage points (not 6%).
A rise from 60% to 66% is 6 percentage points, but a relative increase of 10%. This site writes changes between rates in points.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained (paraphrased) · Related: Employment premium
Post-secondary non-tertiary education (ISCED 4)
Courses after secondary school that are not yet university level, often short vocational programmes.
ISCED level 4. It is small or absent in some countries, so shares at this level can jump around between years.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) (paraphrased) · Related: ISCED, Upper secondary education (ISCED 3), Short-cycle tertiary education (ISCED 5)
Provenance
Where a number comes from: who published it, which dataset, when it was downloaded and under which licence.
Every dataset on Apprentix carries its provenance, including a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the original file, so you can trace and check it.
Source: Apprentix — Data & sources (paraphrased) · Related: CC BY 4.0 licence
Provisional (flag p)
Flag p: an early figure that may still be revised when final data come in.
Source: Eurostat — observation status (flag) code list (paraphrased) · Related: Flags
Purchasing power standard (PPS)
An artificial currency that removes price differences between countries, so one PPS buys the same amount everywhere.
Spending in PPS is fairer to compare than in euros, because prices (and salaries) are much higher in some countries than in others.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: Purchasing power standard (PPS) (paraphrased) · Related: Eurostat

R

Recognition of foreign qualifications also: ENIC-NARIC, foreign VET qualifications
Official checking of a qualification from another country so you can use it to work or study here.
For VET qualifications, who decides varies: ministries, chambers or professional bodies. ENIC-NARIC centres give information and, for academic qualifications, often make the decision. Regulated professions follow EU rules on professional qualifications.
Source: ENIC-NARIC network (paraphrased) · Related: Validation of learning, European Qualifications Framework (EQF)
Recognition of prior learning also: fast-track
Counting what you already know or can do, so you can skip parts of a course or apprenticeship and finish sooner.
It relies on validation of earlier formal, non-formal or informal learning. In apprenticeships it can shorten the duration for adults or people with work experience.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Validation of learning
ReferNet
Cedefop's network of national partners who report on VET in their own country.
Each EU Member State, Iceland and Norway has a ReferNet partner, which writes the national VET system descriptions and policy updates that Cedefop publishes.
Source: Cedefop — ReferNet (paraphrased) · Related: Cedefop

S

School-based VET
Vocational education that happens mostly in a school or training centre, sometimes with short work placements.
In many European countries most VET learners are in school-based programmes. They can include workshops and internships, but the learner is a student rather than an employee.
Source: Cedefop — European database on apprenticeship schemes (paraphrased) · Related: Vocational education and training (VET), Dual system (dual training), Work-based learning
Short-cycle tertiary education (ISCED 5) also: short-cycle higher education, short cycle tertiary
Short higher-education programmes (often two years), usually practical and job-focused, below a bachelor's degree.
ISCED level 5, the first level of tertiary education. Many are vocational, and some countries offer higher apprenticeships at this level.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) (paraphrased) · Related: ISCED, Tertiary education
Skills intelligence
Information on which skills and jobs are in demand now and in future, used to plan education and careers.
Cedefop uses the term for its data and analysis on skill needs: skills forecasts, shortage indicators and the European Skills Index.
Source: Cedefop — Skills intelligence (paraphrased) · Related: Labour and skills shortage index (CLSSI)
Social partners
Organisations that represent employers (business associations) and workers (trade unions).
In many apprenticeship systems social partners help design qualifications and curricula, set apprentice pay through collective agreements, and take part in final exams.
Source: Eurofound — European industrial relations dictionary: Social partners (paraphrased) · Related: Collective agreement, Chambers
Spearman rank correlation (ρ) also: Spearman ρ
A number from −1 to +1 showing whether countries that rank high on one measure also rank high on another.
+1 means identical rankings, 0 means no link, −1 means opposite rankings. Around 0.3–0.5 is a moderate association. A correlation alone does not prove that one thing causes the other.
Source: Apprentix — Insights (method notes) (paraphrased)

T

Tax incentive also: tax incentives, tax deductions
A tax reduction for companies that take on apprentices, to make training cheaper for them.
Source: Cedefop — Financing apprenticeships in the EU (paraphrased) · Related: Training levy, Training fund
Tertiary education also: tertiary-level education
Education after secondary school at college or university level: ISCED 5 (short-cycle) up to 8 (doctorate).
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) (paraphrased) · Related: ISCED, Short-cycle tertiary education (ISCED 5)
Training fund also: training funds
A pot of money, usually filled by employer levies, that pays companies some of the cost of training apprentices or staff.
Funds may be national or run by a sector (often jointly by employers and unions). Cedefop's financing database records them as one type of instrument.
Source: Cedefop — Financing apprenticeships in the EU (paraphrased) · Related: Training levy, Collective agreement
Training levy also: apprenticeship levy, levy fund
A compulsory charge on employers, often a small share of their payroll, used to pay for training and apprenticeships.
The money usually goes into a training fund, which pays it back to companies that train. Examples include the UK apprenticeship levy and France's apprenticeship tax.
Source: Cedefop — Financing apprenticeships in the EU (paraphrased) · Related: Training fund, Tax incentive

U

UNESCO Institute for Statistics (UIS)
UNESCO's statistics office, which collects education data from countries around the world.
Source: UNESCO Institute for Statistics (paraphrased) · Related: ISCED, OECD
Upper secondary education (ISCED 3) also: upper-secondary
The last stage of school, roughly ages 15–18 or 19, after lower secondary. Most apprenticeships start here.
ISCED level 3. It can be general (preparing for university) or vocational. In statistics, 'upper-secondary VET' is the main group of initial VET learners.
Source: Eurostat — Statistics Explained glossary: International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) (paraphrased) · Related: ISCED, Initial VET (IVET), Post-secondary non-tertiary education (ISCED 4)

V

Validation of learning also: validation of non-formal and informal learning
Getting official proof of skills you learned outside school — at work, volunteering or on your own.
Under the 2012 Council Recommendation, validation means an authorised body confirms that a person has learning outcomes measured against a standard. It has four steps: identification, documentation, assessment and certification.
Source: Cedefop — European inventory on validation of non-formal and informal learning (paraphrased) · Related: Recognition of prior learning, Recognition of foreign qualifications
Vocational education and training (VET)
Education that prepares you for a particular job or kind of work, mixing knowledge with hands-on practical skills.
VET can happen in a school, at a workplace or both, and at many ages: as a teenager (initial VET) or later in working life (continuing VET). Apprenticeship is one form of VET, but most VET in Europe is mainly school-based.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Initial VET (IVET), Continuing VET (CVET), Apprenticeship, School-based VET, Work-based learning

W

Work-based learning also: work-based programmes
Learning by actually doing tasks in a real job (or a realistic workshop) and thinking about what you did.
Cedefop defines it as gaining knowledge and skills by carrying out, and reflecting on, tasks in a vocational setting, at a workplace or in a VET institution. The EU target counts recent VET graduates who had at least one month of work-based learning during their programme.
Source: Cedefop — Terminology of European education and training policy (2nd ed., 2014) (paraphrased) · Related: Apprenticeship, EU 2025 targets for VET, School-based VET
