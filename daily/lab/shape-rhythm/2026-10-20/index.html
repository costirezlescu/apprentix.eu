The one thing Debate · 13 October

Two VET scholars debate the capabilities approach in JVET’s 250th issue

Stephanie Allais argues the approach offers VET research little; Leesa Wheelahan argues it has value within broader theories.

Allais says its emphasis on human flourishing is important but not new, that it rests on individualism, and that its outcomes-focused stance “gives weak VET systems new ways to fail”. Wheelahan situates it among critical realism, Bernsteinian sociology and Marxism. Allais replies that “compatibility is not a contribution”. Wheelahan, she notes, accepts that the approach cannot say what a curriculum should contain or diagnose labour-market problems; what remains in dispute is whether its vocabulary helps answer VET’s questions or diverts researchers from more specific theories of knowledge, occupations and the conditions of good work.

Research New paper · cites Cedefop · 13 October

Training pays off in micro-firms too, Research Policy study finds

Using administrative data on training expenses in micro-firms in three Italian regions, the authors find a positive and sizable effect of training on productivity.

Firms with fewer than 10 employees are usually lumped in with larger SMEs, the authors note, and some argue training could even hurt their productivity. The effect held across robustness checks and heterogeneity analysis. The paper mentions Cedefop in its full text.

Research New paper · 13 October

Double mismatch hits graduates hardest, Norwegian survey study finds

In the Norwegian Graduate Survey, 90% of those with horizontal mismatch were also overeducated.

Studying one form of mismatch without the other overstates its effect: the link between horizontal mismatch and job satisfaction falls by 15% once overeducation is controlled for, and the link between overeducation and satisfaction by 45%. For graduates facing both, the negative effect exceeds that of either alone.

Research New paper · 13 October

Swiss apprentices and trainers saw pandemic support differently

A mixed-methods study combined a survey of 1,380 vocational trainers with 16 focus groups involving 119 apprentices.

Trainers tended to report that workplace support was largely maintained. Apprentices described it as less spontaneous and less consistently accessible, and in firms with more workload more often reported limited guidance, feedback and emotional support.

The wider debate Green transition · 16 October

Losing a job in a carbon-intensive sector costs more, German data show

Displaced workers from high-carbon sectors face larger earnings and wage losses than others, a VoxEU column finds from administrative data.

These sectors are regionally concentrated and involve specific occupations and tasks with high firm-wage premia, the authors write, which narrows workers’ options for jobs with similar skills and pay.

Research New paper · cites Cedefop · 15 October

Is overeducation lower in green industries? It depends on the measure

Using PIAAC data for seven Central and Eastern European countries and Cedefop’s 2019 framework for green industries.

Workers’ own assessments show no difference between green and other industries; the statistical measure finds less overeducation in green industries. The authors urge caution, given the broad definition of green industries.

Research New paper · 15 October

Resilience buffers burnout among German VET teachers, study finds

A survey of 1,470 vocational teachers found task scope and complexity to be the most prevalent job demands.

Demands were linked to lower well-being and job satisfaction and more burnout; of the resources studied, only resilience moderated the effect on burnout. The authors suggest reducing demands and building resilience in teacher training.

Paper of the day

Three Chinese counties, three ways to govern vocational training

A comparative study of Longkou, Zhucheng and Pingyi in Shandong finds that local conditions, not national institutions alone, shape how governments, schools and employers organise skill formation.

Xufeng Zou · Journal of Vocational Education and Training, 2 October 2026 · Read the paper

The question
How do uneven local development conditions shape the way vocational education and training is governed within one country?
How it was studied
Comparative case studies of three counties in Shandong Province, treating governance as a configuration of authority, resources, coordination and educational provision.
What it found
Three locally embedded configurations: integrated governance, platform-mediated governance and service-network governance. They differ in how governments, schools and employers coordinate resources and connect educational opportunities with labour markets.
The caveat
Three counties in one province, and an account of how governance is organised rather than of its results for learners.
Why read it
Research on skill formation usually compares national systems. The paper argues that local capacity, institutional history and labour mobility shape it within countries too: the regional lens of Cedefop’s RESET index.

Chart of the day

Do workers’ skills match their jobs? 57% say yes

In 2024, 57% of EU workers answered “My present skills correspond well with my duties”, the same as in 2015 and up from 55% in 2010. The others said they needed further training, or could cope with more demanding duties.

Malta (77%) and Portugal (76%) were highest, Austria lowest (37%); Belgium and Greece were both at 61%.

Data: Cedefop, “Workers with skills matched to their duties (%)”, as published on Apprentix. Chart: Cedefop Daily.
