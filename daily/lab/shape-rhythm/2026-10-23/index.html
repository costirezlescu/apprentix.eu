What world gold cost a young cabinetmaker

Tobias Kelz won WorldSkills gold in Shanghai by a single point, finishing his piece in the last second.

He had 22 hours to build a piece of furniture he first saw on day one, with solid wood, panels, veneer and doors to hang, and made two careless mistakes along the way; his trainer, whom he could only consult in breaks, found a way to recover. In the evenings they negotiated every minute of the next day’s time.

The road began after his journeyman’s exam, more than two years earlier: the chamber, Bavarian and national competitions, then a year of training with the other national winners before the trainers picked two cabinetmakers for Shanghai. In the last six months there was no real free time: a week a month at the wood technical school in Bad Wildungen, work by day, the workshop after hours. Some days, he says, he asked himself why he was doing it; his employer accepted many absences.

What he takes away: new techniques, more speed, and more calm. He would do it again, he says, but not a second time.
