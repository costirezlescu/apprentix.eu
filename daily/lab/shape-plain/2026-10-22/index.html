The one thing AI and work · 16 October

Irish tech union grows five-fold amid AI-driven layoffs

While Dublin hosted the presidency’s International AI Summit, a Workers’ AI Counter Summit met the weekend before, Politico Europe reports.

The Digital and Techworker Alliance, founded in 2023, had around 270 members in its first year and a half and now has more than 1,350, its organiser told the outlet. Ireland counts more than 170,000 tech workers; Meta has roughly halved its Irish workforce since the pandemic, and TikTok plans to cut around 300 Irish jobs, mostly in trust and safety. An IMF report this year found Ireland’s labour market “relatively more exposed” to AI than many advanced economies.

AI watch

Workplace learning

Learning about AI left hospital trainers less sure of themselves

A study at Montreal’s university hospital describes a “knowing-doing gap”: awareness of AI tools grew faster than their use.

What happened
Six healthcare professionals who train colleagues at the Centre hospitalier de l’Université de Montréal followed an eight-week programme of pedagogical guides and microlearning videos on AI, then discussed it in two focus groups.
What they found
More knowledge came with less confidence. Participants drew lines between appropriate and inappropriate uses of AI, and tool access, protected time and institutional endorsement shaped how far their AI literacy developed.
What it means for skills
The authors read lower confidence as a sign of more differentiated professional judgment rather than failure, and argue that individual learning needs an infrastructure of agency. Their findings, they write, challenge adoption-centric models of AI literacy.

Chart of the day

Six in ten EU adults have at least basic digital skills

In 2025, 60.4% of 16–74-year-olds in the EU had at least basic digital skills, up from 53.9% in 2021: the ground on which any AI literacy has to be built.

The Netherlands (83.6%) and Ireland (82.8%) led; Romania was lowest (31.8%). Belgium stood at 61.2%.

Data: Cedefop, “Adults (16-74 year-olds) with at least basic digital skills (%)”, as published on Apprentix. Chart: Cedefop Daily.
