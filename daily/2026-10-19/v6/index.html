The one thing New publication · 15 October

Cedefop’s new RESET index scores skills in all 244 EU regions

Published on 15 October, the regional skills ecosystems index compares regions on skills supply, demand, environment and match. Central Macedonia, home to the Thessaloniki office, ranks 208th; Brussels-Capital ranks 101st.

Cedefop describes RESET as the first multidimensional EU regional index focused specifically on skills and VET, with 37 indicators across four dimensions. Scores run from 1 to 100 relative to the other regions (version July 2026, reference year 2024): Stockholm is highest at 75.9, Sud-Vest Oltenia lowest at 26.8. Central Macedonia scores 36.5, fourth of 13 Greek regions; its strongest dimension is skills match (55.3), its weakest skills demand (23.6). Each region also has scores on indicators for people with initial VET: their job tasks, activity, employment and unemployment.

Executive Director Jürgen Siebel said the first event “confirmed how strong the demand is for evidence that reflects territorial realities”.

From the field England · 17 October

England plans to charge firms more to train over-25s than young apprentices

Ministers are considering raising the cost of training a worker over 25 by 25% under the growth and skills levy, the Guardian reports, to reverse a 40% fall in youth apprenticeship starts over a decade.

More than half of new starts are now for over-25s, and under-19 enrolments have almost halved in a decade. The plan adds a “co-investment” rule to the £4bn-a-year levy as part of the response to Alan Milburn’s review of youth unemployment, which, the paper reported a week earlier, will call for a “national pre-apprenticeship route”.

At Cedefop New publication · 30 September

34 apprenticeship schemes show incremental quality gains, Cedefop finds

Published on 30 September, five years after Cedefop’s previous analysis of the European framework for quality and effective apprenticeships and ahead of the forthcoming European VET strategy.

Criteria on learning and working conditions are usually addressed; on framework conditions the picture is mixed, with several indicators unevenly met. Across criteria, the report finds that where social partners or chambers take a fuller part in decision-making and coordination, they also invest more in implementation, and limited roles go with narrower coverage of quality criteria.

At Cedefop Joint report · 15 October

AI literacy is a new basic skill, inter-agency report argues

Written within the Inter-agency TVET group on skill mismatch in digitalised labour markets, with inputs from Cedefop.

It finds AI adoption raising demand for higher-order cognitive and socioemotional skills and for digital and data science skills, and a small but fast-growing market for jobs building and maintaining AI systems. Everyone now needs the ability to use AI tools safely and ethically, it argues, and skills policies should prioritise broad capabilities and human agency.

At Cedefop Country support · 15 October

Italy and Czechia join Cedefop project on skills for the age of AI

WORK.FUTURE, launched in Rome on 22 September, is a two-year project under the EU’s Technical Support Instrument with the Commission’s SG REFORM and both national authorities.

Cedefop will analyse up to five occupations or sectors in each country, with foresight workshops and regional roundtables. The newsletter’s comparisons: 26% of employment in both countries was in high-skilled jobs in 2023, against 38% in the EU; adult learning participation was 29% in Italy and 21% in Czechia, against 39%.

Agencies Cedefop and EU-OSHA · 6 October

Safer jobs can help ease labour shortages, say Cedefop and EU-OSHA

A joint article sets EU-OSHA evidence on working conditions beside Cedefop’s intelligence on skills and labour shortages.

Shortage occupations it names include welders, electricians, plumbers, cooks, truck drivers and health care assistants, many with demanding or hazardous work. In EU-OSHA’s 2025 OSH Pulse survey, 29% of workers reported stress, anxiety or depression caused or worsened by work, up from 27% in 2022.

Chart of the day

Recent VET graduates in work: the EU at 80.1%, short of its 82% target

In 2025, 80.1% of 20–34-year-olds who had finished vocational upper-secondary or post-secondary education one to three years earlier were in work, up from 75.9% in 2020. The EU target was at least 82% by 2025.

Germany led with 91.5%. Belgium stood at 75.4%, and Greece, at 58.0%, was lowest among the countries with data.

Data: Eurostat, “Employment rate of recent VET graduates”, as published on Apprentix. Chart: Cedefop Daily.
