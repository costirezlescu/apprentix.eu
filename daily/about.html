How this paper is made

Cedefop Daily is a morning paper on what Cedefop works on: vocational education and training, apprenticeships, skills, qualifications, adult learning and the future of work. It also follows the EU institutions, sister agencies, member states and researchers whose work touches those areas. It is an independent publication on Apprentix and is not published or endorsed by Cedefop.

Five rules

  1. Relevant, or it doesn’t run. Under every story, a line says which of Cedefop’s work areas it touches. That line is generated from the source data by a template, never written by hand. If no line can be generated, the story is left out.
  2. Sourced. Every story links to its source. Figures are copied from the source, never recalculated into new claims. Quotations are checked word for word against the source.
  3. Neutral. The paper reports and does not editorialise. Evaluations are attributed to whoever made them. It never speaks for Cedefop.
  4. Short. Sections appear only when they have something worth running. A quiet day makes a shorter paper.
  5. Discreet about people. Nobody is searched for. People appear only in a public role, as named by an official source or as authors of the papers covered. News about colleagues will run only if it comes through approved channels, with consent and an opt-out.

How an edition is built

  1. Fetch. A script reads public sources over plain HTTP: Cedefop’s RSS feed and newsletter, the EU Publications Office catalogue, the Commission’s press corner, the Council’s press feed, the Official Journal, the news pages of EU and UN agencies, Eurostat, OpenAlex for research, Apprentix’s own data, and the press (below). A source that refuses automated reading is recorded as refused and skipped. Bot checks are never worked around.
  2. Tag. Each item is tagged with Cedefop work areas by keyword, in several languages.
  3. Write. Stories are drafted from the fetched text only, with help from software that includes a language model. Links, dates and the line under each story come from the data, not from the writing step.
  4. Check. Before publication, every link must come from the fetched sources, every number must appear in the story’s sources, and every quotation must be verbatim. Banned words, sensitive topics (which hold the edition for a person to decide), a person in more than one headline and past events in the diary are flagged.
  5. Publish. The checked edition is rendered as a static page. Each edition’s sources are listed in its sources.json.

Not read: Cedefop website pages (refuse scripts (bot check); the RSS feed is used instead); Council of the EU pages (refuse scripts; only the press-release feed is used); Eurofound news (rate-limited (429)); OECD (refuses scripts (403)); Media monitoring (not connected: no access yet).

The press and the field

To see how things are on the ground, not only from the institutions, the paper reads national and local newspapers, trade press, teachers’, learners’ and employers’ outlets, international newspapers and magazines, popular science and economics columns. An article is kept only if it touches education, training and skills, or AI together with work and jobs, and only if it is from the last eight days.

Press articles are summarised in the paper’s own words and linked; at most a short phrase is quoted. Articles in other languages are summarised in English and labelled “translated from …”. Private individuals in press stories are not named; people appear only in a public role. Subscription articles are marked.

Sections

About thirteen items a day, a five- to seven-minute read: The one thing (the day’s most important story, with a line on why it matters to Cedefop), Six to know, the weekday feature, the Chart of the day, Today’s question (a number to guess, a myth to call, a fact or an insight), the Diary, and More today as a short list of links. On Fridays, Lives opens the paper: one real person whose life vocational education and training has shaped. Stories come from Cedefop, the field, the wider debate, EU policy, the agencies and research, each marked with its section colour. The top strip shows “Today at Cedefop” when there is a public agenda for the day, and “This week in numbers” otherwise.

Each weekday has a focus and a standing feature: Monday EU policy and Cedefop, with Deadlines (Commission initiatives open for feedback that touch Cedefop’s work, from the “Have your say” portal) and the week ahead in the diary; Tuesday research, with the Paper of the day (one new paper in plain language: question, method, finding, caveat); Wednesday the field, with Country in focus (one country’s numbers, regions, recent VET policy and typical apprentices); Thursday AI and the future of work, with AI watch; Friday long reads, with Lives, From the archive (what Cedefop published this week years ago, and what has changed since) and Out this week: concerts, films, talks and exhibitions in Thessaloniki for the week ahead, picked from the venues’ own listings. Each date, time and price is checked against the venue’s page; check the link before you go.

Every day, the Chart of the day draws one indicator from the data Apprentix publishes (Cedefop, Eurostat and national sources). The chart is generated from the data; the two lines beside it are checked against it.

Lives

Lives stories come from published profiles and interviews. A person is named only as printed, and only when the source is a profile or interview they chose to give, in which they are the subject. Minors are never named, and nothing about health, family or money is included beyond what the story itself is about.

Privacy

No cookies, no tracking and no analytics. If you guess The number, your guess is kept only in your browser.

Corrections

Every version of every edition stays online, exactly as it was published: when an edition is corrected or extended, the earlier version moves to its own address and is listed under Editions. Errors are corrected in the next edition, in the same place. To report one, open an issue.
